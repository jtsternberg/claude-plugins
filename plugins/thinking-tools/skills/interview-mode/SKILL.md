# Interview Mode

> Two guesses missed. The third guess is not the answer — the question is.

## The Principle

After two rejected or corrected attempts at the same artifact, the problem is no longer the
draft. It's the model of what the user wants, and generating a third variant from that same
wrong model is just a slower way to be wrong. Stop producing and start asking.

Two sentences of why: two failed guesses are evidence the internal model is wrong, and no
amount of rewriting fixes a wrong model. A specific question costs the user seconds; a wrong
third draft costs them a full review cycle.

## When This Skill Activates

- The user says "interview me", "interview mode", "stop guessing", "ask me instead"
- The **second** rejection, rewrite, or substantive correction of the same artifact
- The user rewrites your draft themselves rather than telling you what to change
- The artifact is voice-sensitive from the outset — email sent as the user, a bio, a public
  post, client-facing copy, anything signed with their name
- Naming and positioning work where the "right" answer is a taste call only the user holds

## The Protocol

### Step 1: Announce the switch — one line

"Switching to interview mode — asking instead of guessing." No apology, no preamble, no
retrospective on the failed drafts.

### Step 2: One question at a time

Ask a single question, then wait for the answer. A batch of five questions gets one shallow
answer to the easiest one.

### Step 3: Tune every question to pull specifics, stories, and point of view

Generic prompts ("what tone are you going for?") get generic answers. Aim at concrete
material and at the user's own phrasing:

- "Say it to me the way you'd say it to them."
- "What's the one thing this has to land, even if the rest gets cut?"
- "Where did this actually happen — what did it cost?"
- "What would you say to someone who flatly disagrees with you here?"
- "What's the version of this you'd never put in writing?"

### Step 4: Alternate two interviewer instincts

A **warm story-puller** draws out the scene, the detail, the feeling. A **blunt
pushback-giver** challenges the claim and makes the user defend it. Swap between them so
the interview both opens up and pressure-tests. Staying in one register gets either a pile
of soft material or a defensive user.

### Step 5: Read through dictation noise

Answers may arrive as raw voice transcripts — mis-transcriptions, run-ons, no punctuation.
Interpret through the noise. On genuine ambiguity, ask a short clarifying follow-up rather
than building the artifact on a misread.

### Step 6: Stop at enough — usually 3–6 questions

Enough material to build from, not a complete brief. The user ends it whenever they want
("enough", "go", "wrap it") — honor that immediately, even mid-question.

### Step 7: Build FROM the answers

Their words are the material. Select, order, and stitch what they said; keep connective
tissue minimal. Shape clay — don't invent new claims, metaphors, or phrasings and attribute
them to the user's intent.

Where the call is genuinely taste — openers, closings, names, subject lines — present
**2–3 options** instead of one guess. Those are the spots where a single choice is another
guess in disguise.

## Quick Diagnostic

1. How many attempts at this artifact have already been corrected? (Two → interview.)
2. Am I about to change wording without knowing what was wrong with the last wording?
3. Does the finished artifact contain sentences the user actually said?
4. Am I choosing for them in a spot where only their taste can decide?
